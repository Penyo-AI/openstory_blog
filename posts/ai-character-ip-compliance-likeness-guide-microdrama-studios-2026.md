Vertical microdramas are now a large business. Media Partners Asia expects microdrama revenue outside China to reach [$3.6 billion in 2026](https://www.screendaily.com/news/microdrama-revenue-outside-china-to-hit-36bn-in-2026-says-report/5219165.article), and it estimates that market leader ReelShort will cross $1 billion in revenue this year, as [Evan Shapiro's analysis](https://eshap.substack.com/p/micodramas-are-a-tiny-part-of-the) of the MPA report describes. That money brings scrutiny from distributors, telecom partners, guilds and rights holders.

For a studio making AI microdramas, one of the biggest avoidable risks to a 60-episode release is **likeness drift**: a synthetic lead who slowly comes to look like a real actor or a famous character. This guide explains why it happens, what the 2026 rules say, and how to screen a cast before a single episode is rendered.

## Why Unscreened AI Casts Are a Distribution Risk

```text
Generic prompt: "charismatic 30-year-old CEO in a charcoal suit"
        │
        ▼
Model falls back on faces it has seen most often
        │
        ▼
Unscreened lead starts to resemble a real public figure
        │
        ▼
Risk: takedown or recast mid-season → re-render every shot with that character
```

When a prompt relies on generic descriptions like *"a handsome 30-year-old tech billionaire"* or *"a fierce action heroine in a leather jacket"*, image and video models have little to anchor on and can drift toward familiar faces from their training data. Without a check in pre-production, a lead can end up looking like a Hollywood actor or a franchise character. If that is spotted at Episode 25, every shot with that character has to be redone, or the series comes down from ReelShort, DramaBox or ShortMax.

### 1. SAG-AFTRA: Digital Replicas and Synthetic Performers

SAG-AFTRA members ratified the [2026 TV/Theatrical Agreement](https://www.sagaftra.org/contracts-industry-resources/contracts/2026-tvtheatrical-contracts) in June, effective July 1, 2026 through June 30, 2030. [The Hollywood Reporter's summary](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/business/business-news/sag-aftra-four-year-deal-studios-new-ai-protections-1236590891/) and [GenHQ's breakdown](https://join.genhq.io/post/sag-aftra-2026-ai-actors-synthetic-performers) highlight the terms that matter for AI casting:

- **Synthetic performers:** producers commit to "a principle strongly favoring human performances." Before using a synthetic performer in a role a human would otherwise play, the producer must give the union written notice and bargain, and the synthetic must bring "significant additional value."
- **Digital replicas:** using a real performer's digital replica requires consent, and the deal sets minimum payments and residuals for independently created replicas. Producers also need an "articulable business reason" to scan a performer.

SAG-AFTRA also has a separate [Verticals and New Media agreement](https://www.sagaftra.org/production-center/contract/884/getting-started) for short-form work. If you hire union talent for any part of a show, assume these rules reach your AI assets too, and get specific advice from an entertainment lawyer.

### 2. Writers Guild Coverage Reaches Microdramas

In November 2026, writers at DramaWave became the first full-time microdrama writing staff represented by the Writers Guild of America, as the [Los Angeles Times](https://www.latimes.com/entertainment-arts/business/story/2026-11-16/writers-guild-represents-microdrama-writers-for-first-time) and [TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/culture-lifestyle/culture/writers-guild-unionizes-first-microdrama-studio/) reported. It is a sign that vertical drama is moving into mainstream Hollywood labor practice, where credits and the provenance of scripts and assets get checked.

### 3. Southeast Asia: Distribution Deals, AI Labels and Voice Work

Southeast Asia is a fast-growing market for vertical series. [KOFICE's research report](https://kofice.or.kr/pic-hub/research/post.php?id=res4&lang=en) notes that ReelShort signed distribution deals with AIS in Thailand, Globe in the Philippines, Telkomsel in Indonesia and Singtel in Singapore, and that DataEye expects AI-generated and animated dramas to make up nearly 70% of the overseas microdrama market in 2026. The same report flags two policy changes to watch:

- **AI labeling:** Thailand's draft AI Act, revised in July 2026, would require AI-generated images, audio and video to carry machine-readable marks and platforms to display labels.
- **Voice work:** Indonesia has recognized voice-over as a creative-economy subsector in its new master plan, which puts the protection of voice actors against AI substitutes on the policy agenda.

Telecom partners and platforms carrying your show will want to know who and what is on screen. A documented casting process makes those conversations easier.

### Step 1: Draw Original Multi-View Casting Cards

Start in [AI Character Casting](https://plotparty.ai/tools/ai-character-casting). Write a brief, add reference photos, or both, then pick one of two formats:

1. **Half-Body Portrait:** head to waist, for dialogue close-ups, two-shots and quick auditions.
2. **16:9 Casting Card:** three views on one canvas:
   - a full-body front view that fixes posture, costume and proportions;
   - a full-body back view with the face hidden, which fixes hair length, silhouette and rear wardrobe without making the video model paint a second face on the back of the head;
   - a high-detail face close-up for eye color, skin texture and facial structure.

Casting uses Gemini 3 Pro Image by default, and you can switch to any image model in Plot Party, including [Nano Banana 2](https://plotparty.ai/nano-banana/2), [GPT Image 2.5](https://plotparty.ai/gpt-image/2.5) or [Midjourney](https://plotparty.ai/midjourney). Need a new outfit for Episode 10? Use the approved face close-up as the reference photo and describe the new wardrobe.

```text
┌──────────────────────┬──────────────────────┬──────────────────────┐
│ Full-body front view │ Full-body back view  │ Face close-up        │
│ posture, outfit      │ hair, silhouette     │ eyes, skin, features │
└──────────────────────┴──────────────────────┴──────────────────────┘
 Character IP check: not flagged → ready to attach
```

### Step 2: Run the Character IP Check

Before any video is generated, run the **Character IP check** on the card. It asks AI whether the character looks like a known IP character or celebrity and shows a similarity flag, for example *"94% similar · flagged in casting."*

If a card is flagged, edit it instead of starting over. Open the card in [GPT Image 2.5](https://plotparty.ai/gpt-image/2.5) or Nano Banana 2 and change a few defining features (jawline, hairstyle, eyebrow shape, wardrobe colors) while keeping the rest of the design, then check it again. The IP check is a screening tool that catches lookalikes early; it does not replace a legal review for a commercial release.

### Step 3: Attach the Approved Card Across Sets and Video Models

Once a card clears, it stays in your workspace as the character's reference for the rest of production:

- **In 3D sets:** inside a [3D Snowglobe](https://plotparty.ai/tools/ai-3d-world-generator) room, place characters with a prompt and bind their approved casting cards, so every camera angle shows the same person in the same space.
- **In video shots:** attach the card as `@Reference` in [Seedance 2.5](https://plotparty.ai/seedance/2.5) or [Seedance 2.5 4K](https://plotparty.ai/seedance/2.5-4k), or as a fully preserved subject in [MiniMax H3](https://plotparty.ai/minimax/h3). [Consistent Character Video](https://plotparty.ai/tools/consistent-character-video) keeps the same face across shots without training a custom model.

Check face, costume and proportions in every approved shot, whichever model rendered it.

### Step 4: Keep Voices and Localization Clean

Voice deserves the same care as faces. Use synthetic voices from the platform rather than a clone of a real actor's voice you have no rights to. [MiniMax H3](https://plotparty.ai/minimax/h3) generates lip-synced dialogue in 11 languages, and [AI Subtitles & Dubbing](https://plotparty.ai/tools/ai-subtitles-and-dubbing) re-voices up to 20 finished episodes per batch into 40 languages while keeping the original music and sound effects. If a distribution partner asks how your voices were made, you have one clear answer.

## 3 Copyable Prompts for Original, IP-Safe Microdrama Leads

**Prompt 1: an original CEO romance lead**, for [AI Character Casting](https://plotparty.ai/tools/ai-character-casting) with the 16:9 card format.

```text
Standardized 16:9 three-view character casting card, ultra-high-resolution studio lighting, neutral plain gray background.

Character: original 29-year-old female tech entrepreneur of East Asian descent. Distinctive features: sharp asymmetric bob with subtle burgundy highlights, almond-shaped dark brown eyes, natural skin texture with light freckles across the bridge of the nose, high cheekbones. Outfit: tailored emerald-green minimalist blazer over a matte black silk blouse, dark trousers.

Layout: left, full-body front view; middle, full-body back view showing hair shape and blazer tailoring with the face hidden; right, detailed facial close-up. Photorealistic, clean lighting, original non-derivative features, no resemblance to any real celebrity.
```

**Prompt 2: editing a flagged card**, for [GPT Image 2.5](https://plotparty.ai/gpt-image/2.5) with the flagged card attached as `@Reference1`.

```text
Edit casting card @Reference1 so the character no longer resembles any known person.

Keep the clothing style, body posture and three-view 16:9 layout. Change the hair from straight dark brown to a layered, shoulder-length wavy cut in deep espresso. Soften the jawline from sharp and square to an oval contour. Set the eyes slightly wider apart and give the eyebrows a soft arch. Keep the original skin tone and the emerald-green blazer.

Output an updated, consistent 16:9 card with the full-body front view, full-body back view (face hidden) and face close-up.
```

**Prompt 3: a two-character 9:16 shot**, for [Seedance 2.5](https://plotparty.ai/seedance/2.5) or [Seedance 2.5 4K](https://plotparty.ai/seedance/2.5-4k) with both approved cards attached.

```text
Vertical 9:16 dramatic shot, cinematic 24fps.

A dimly lit luxury hotel lobby. The female lead (@Reference1, emerald-green blazer) faces the male lead (@Reference2, 34-year-old architect in a navy wool coat with silver-rimmed glasses). Over-the-shoulder medium shot on @Reference1's reaction as @Reference2 hands her a sealed envelope.

Warm ambient lighting, shallow depth of field softening the marble pillars behind them. Keep both characters' faces and outfits exactly as on their casting cards @Reference1 and @Reference2. Realistic fabric and skin detail.
```
